Approach

How the house breathes

Bioclimatic design is not a style. It is physics applied to a plot. This page explains what we do and why it works.

Why we do not clone global designs

A house is a machine for its climate. The glass box that works under a grey northern sky becomes an oven at 36° latitude. For decades Algerian houses copied plans drawn for other places, then bought air conditioning to survive them.

Our vernacular already solved this. The Kasbah of Algiers stays cool through narrow shaded alleys and thick walls. The ksour of the Sahara use mass, courtyards, and wind towers. We do not reproduce their ornament. We reproduce their physics, then measure it.

Low-tech, not low-quality. Most of what we do is orientation, proportion, and placement. It does not require a fantasy budget. It requires decisions made before the first wall goes up.

The five principles

What every plan is checked against

  1. 1

    Cross-ventilation

    Low inlets on the cool side, high outlets on the warm side. Air moves because pressure differs, not because a window is open.

  2. 2

    The courtyard stack

    A central void lets warm air rise and leave. The patio is the lung of the house, not a leftover space.

  3. 3

    Thermal mass, used on purpose

    Thick walls store the day's heat and give it back at night. We place mass where night air can flush it.

  4. 4

    Brise-soleil by the sun angle

    Shading is calculated from latitude and season so summer sun stays out and winter sun comes in.

  5. 5

    Living facades

    Local succulents and a light soil layer cool the wall surface and the air that passes it.

Deeper

Three effects we design with

Venturi effect

Air speeds up when it passes through a narrower opening. We size inlets smaller than outlets so a slow breeze becomes a felt draft indoors.

Stack effect

Warm air rises. A stairwell or courtyard void with a high outlet turns the whole house into a quiet chimney that runs all night.

Thermal lag

Thick walls delay heat by hours. Placed correctly, the day's heat reaches the interior after sunset, when night air can carry it away.

Light and the body

Daylight bounced deep into a room does more than save electricity. It tells your body what time it is.Field Notes, episode 05

Light shelves push daylight onto the ceiling and deep into the plan, so rooms are bright without glare. Views to the sky and horizon are placed on purpose. Bedrooms get morning light and evening shade. Kitchens get task light above 90 CRI so colour reads true.

We design for circadian rhythm because a house is where people sleep, wake, and recover.

Method

How a project runs

  1. Site and climate reading

    Sun path, prevailing wind, neighbours, and shade. We measure before we draw.

  2. Strategy

    A one-page climate strategy: where mass goes, where air enters and leaves, what is shaded and when.

  3. Design

    Plans, sections, and details that carry the strategy. Every opening has a reason.

  4. Build and verify

    Site supervision, then a summer visit to confirm the house behaves as drawn.
