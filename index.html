How the house breathes

Five principles behind every plan

None of them need a fantasy budget. They need decisions made early and made correctly.

  1. 1

    Cross-ventilation

    Low inlets on the cool side, high outlets on the warm side. Air moves because pressure differs, not because a window is open.

  2. 2

    The courtyard stack

    A central void lets warm air rise and leave. The patio is the lung of the house, not a leftover space.

  3. 3

    Thermal mass, used on purpose

    Thick walls store the day's heat and give it back at night. We place mass where night air can flush it.

  4. 4

    Brise-soleil by the sun angle

    Shading is calculated from latitude and season so summer sun stays out and winter sun comes in.

  5. 5

    Living facades

    Local succulents and a light soil layer cool the wall surface and the air that passes it.

Questions

What clients ask first

Short answers here. Longer ones in a conversation.

Does a bioclimatic house cost more to build?

Usually not. Most of the gains come from orientation, wall thickness, opening placement, and shading, which are decisions, not products. Where we do specify extra elements such as a brise-soleil or a green roof, we cost them against the air conditioning they replace.

Do you work outside Algiers?

Yes. The studio has designed houses on the coast, in the Mitidja, and in the south. Each climate zone gets its own strategy; a house in Ghardaïa is not a house in Tipaza.

Can you improve an existing house?

Often, yes. Retrofits such as external shading, light shelves, a planted facade, or opening up a stairwell for the stack effect can change a house without rebuilding it. We start with a site visit and an honest assessment.
